20 Aug Land is trading again — SOLYD buys an Alta de Lisboa plot for ~300 homes, and the rehab-VAT fight is settledLatest ▾

The first named Lisbon land trade in over a week: SOLYD Property Developers acquired a ~10,000 m² lot in Alta de Lisboa to build roughly 300 homes across four blocks — a concrete buildable-land comp after a run of deal-less days. New law Lei 48/2026 settled the long-running ARU/ORU controversy, confirming the 6% rehabilitation VAT applies on ARU location alone and reaching back retroactively — a clean build-cost win for value-add. On the flip side, a Novo Banco–affiliated fund's 683-home Campolide scheme is now before the Public Prosecutor over alleged master-plan and environmental breaches, and Brussels opened infraction proceedings against Portugal for missing the EPBD energy deadline. A development-and-green day: buildable land and rehab economics both improved, while planning-litigation and compliance risk both stepped up.

19 Aug The affordability squeeze becomes the market's defining story as prices set a ninth straight record ▾

Fresh idealista data put national rents at 82% of net household income and purchase costs at 76% — more than double the 33% comfort threshold — even as asking prices hit a ninth consecutive record at €3,210/m² (+8% YoY). Into that tension, Banco de Portugal's tighter mortgage rule took effect, cutting the debt-service cap from 50% to 45% and threatening to shave 10–15% off new-loan approvals. The state is answering on supply with 28,000 PRR public homes due by month-end, but that capital sits in the affordable segment, leaving mid-market, prime and rental to private players. The widening wedge — owner-occupiers priced out, institutional and rental capital stepping in — keeps strengthening the build-to-rent and value-add case.

17 Aug A quiet week consolidates around rental-law reform and the new 90-day short-let cap ▾

The most consequential item remained the rental-law reform bill before Parliament: automatic renewals would match the original contract term rather than defaulting to three years, landlords could oppose the first renewal immediately, and — directly relevant to yield strategy — tourism sub-letting of a dwelling would be capped at 90 days a year. It sits against a market where H1 commercial investment reached €1.42bn (+11%, hotel-led) and Q1 house prices hit a record €2,337/m². Marquee development signals were unchanged — Coporgest's €220m Park Hyatt Comporta and Panattoni's Valongo logistics lease — with Porto office take-up up ~80% as Lisbon cools. The policy stack keeps tilting away from short-let yield and toward long-lease, BTR and development.

16 Aug Rental law reaches Parliament; the CIA and RSAA rental regimes go live in two weeks ▾

The government's rewrite of the rental law landed in Parliament with granular, landlord-favourable terms: the automatic renewal floor drops from three years to one, owners can oppose the very first renewal, and the notice to recover a home for deep works is cut from five years to three. Underneath it, the RSAA and 25-year CIA fiscal regimes go live on 1 September — CIA being the first Portuguese instrument purpose-built for institutional buy-to-rent, offering 25 years of tax certainty. The freshest market prints showed record H1 revenue at every major brokerage against a 10.5% drop in Q1 transactions — a supply-starved, price-led market. The case for a CIA-structurable build-to-rent sleeve strengthened again.

15 Aug Countdown to the 1 September launch of the RSAA and CIA rental regimes ▾

Two weeks out from the launch of the RSAA and CIA regimes under Decreto-Lei 97/2026, the policy-versus-price split defining the summer sharpened. RSAA grants a full IRS exemption on rents at or below 80% of local median, while CIA offers institutional investors 25-year fiscal-stability contracts, layered on the package's already-live 10% rental-income rate and 6% construction VAT. Prices held a ninth straight record (€3,210/m², +8%) but cooled and diverged hard by region — Algarve +17%, Porto +8.4%, Lisbon just +4.1% — while Euribor rose across all tenors. Confidence-side prints were strong: record H1 revenue at the big brokerage networks and a 12% jump in new construction-company formations. The incentive structure is tilting decisively toward long-term rental supply.

14 Aug Express rentals and cooling sale prices split the market in two ▾

The rental and sales markets diverged hard. Fresh data showed 7% of rental listings gone in under 24 hours — Évora above 40% — a vivid read on rental undersupply, just as the 2026 Housing Package's core tax breaks (6% construction VAT, 10% rental-income rate, IMT relief for build-to-rent) sat newly in force. The sales side did the opposite: Q2 asking prices −3.4% QoQ to €3,544/m² with time-to-sell up 54% to 184 days, even as one in ten homes still sold within a week. INE inflation eased to 3.0%, softening the rate outlook; Corum publicly reaffirmed appetite for Portuguese logistics and the €60m half-stake in Acqua Portimão stood out as a large Algarve ticket. A softening entry point paired with policy tailwinds and structurally deep rental demand.

12 Aug Algarve ultra-prime breaks €12,000/m² as the 12-month Euribor ticks to a fresh high ▾

The top end of the Algarve entered a new price tier: Quinta do Lago at €12,793/m², Castro Marim at €12,626/m², and Loulé up 19% year-on-year to €10,804/m², with premium villas routinely at €10,000–€12,000/m². Financing edged the other way, the 12-month Euribor rising to a fresh 2.913% high, even as the six-month benchmark most Portuguese mortgages track eased. The national picture was unchanged — asking prices at a ninth straight record but cooling in Lisbon while the interior compounds ~15% — with no fresh regulatory movement. A quiet mid-August day whose two movers, ultra-prime pricing and the rate uptick, cut the same way: the exit ceiling keeps rising while the cost of reaching it does too.

11 Aug A fiscal-versus-luxury split-screen: incentives reward moderate supply while capital concentrates in prime ▾

A split-screen day. The government's housing-supply tax package (DL 97/2026) drew fresh scrutiny — a 6% construction VAT on moderate-price homes capped at €660,982, a 5% tax on affordable-lease fund income, and a new build-to-rent CIA regime with IMT/IMI relief — a direct margin lever, but one that caps eligible product below the prime band. Meanwhile the luxury market pulled the other way: 80% of Portugal's 19,000-plus €1m homes now sit in Lisbon, Faro and Porto, with Setúbal emerging in the €3m-plus tier. International prime demand kept validating the top end, with Knight Frank projecting Lisbon prime prices +4.5% in 2026, ahead of Paris, London and Zurich. The incentives reward moderate-price supply while scarcity keeps concentrating in the prime coastal-urban poles — an argument for structuring around both.

10 Aug Golden Visa fund inflows hold up despite the tighter citizenship track; the Algarve living pipeline deepens ▾

A quiet summer Monday whose most fund-relevant signal was on the capital-raising side: Golden Visa-eligible funds pulled in €283m of fresh subscriptions between January and May — roughly three times redemptions — even after the nationality law tightened the citizenship track from five to ten years, evidence the fund route is proving resilient. On the ground the Algarve hospitality and living pipeline kept deepening, with IHG signing the 323-key InterContinental Vilamoura and Finangeste's €300m, 1,461-home mixed-use scheme advancing in Faro. Fundamentals were unchanged — record prices at €3,210/m², the Algarve now the priciest rental region, and H1 commercial investment confirmed at €1.42bn. Nothing forced a re-underwrite; resilient fund-route capital and a strengthening Algarve bid simply reinforced the thesis.
